THE RESEARCH QUESTION

A tax base frozen in 1991.
A housing market that has moved on.

England’s Council Tax bands still reflect property values on 1 April 1991. Since then, housing markets have changed unevenly across places and price segments. What does this mean for the tax people face relative to a property’s current value—and what would different reforms change?

The accompanying paper follows three questions: how closely historical bands reflect current values, how tax burdens vary across properties and places, and who gains or loses under reform. This atlas brings the tax-burden and reform evidence together: start with the rules, explore the national picture, then look closely at an area.

The central finding: reclassifying properties is only part of reform. Updating the relative charging weights, especially alongside rebanding, produces larger reductions in measured regressivity than rebanding alone.

07 / DATA & METHODS

What these numbers mean

This atlas accompanies research on England’s frozen Council Tax base. It presents existing analysis results and aggregate calculations from the saved analysis samples.

Prepared by Sofie R. Waltl and Shizhen Wang · Research preview, 29 October 2026

Samples and the effective tax rate

ETR = 100 × standard annual liability / observed sale price. Only observations strictly above each year’s 99.5th current-ETR percentile are excluded. Ties and the entire lower tail are retained. There is no price trimming, winsorisation or policy-specific filtering. Transactions, including repeated sales, are not a stock census.

Reform rules and revenue neutrality

Options 1, 2, 4, 5 and 6 preserve baseline revenue separately in each LAD transaction sample; Option 3 adds a surcharge. Prices and behaviour are held fixed. Rebanding targets official September-2026 local stock shares retrospectively, using fixed LAD2026 groups. Local median-price multipliers are not themselves bill changes; revenue rescaling also matters. High-value thresholds use the saved April-2026 uprating assumptions.

Geography, missing values and colour scales

All 33,755 English LSOA2021 areas remain in the map, including no-sale areas. LSOA display geometry is simplified at 50 metres; the statistical values are unchanged. All 296 LAD2026 result codes match the retained LAD2025 reference polygons; this verifies code coverage rather than identical boundary vintages. For historical LAD Explorer summaries, unique LSOA-to-LAD links come from the retained August-2026 postcode lookup. Ambiguous areas are excluded from local summaries and remain in national totals; they are not assigned arbitrarily. Current-level colour classes are pooled across years; policy-change scales are fixed across years and policies. Saturated end colours include changes beyond the legend endpoints.

Limits to interpretation

Current band snapshots do not provide complete historical assignment records. Standard liabilities exclude household discounts, exemptions, support and premiums. Neighbourhood income has incomplete denominator provenance; deprivation uses fixed Census2021 attributes. No causal tax-price effects, household-income incidence, bootstrap uncertainty or full-stock revenue neutrality are implied.

Sources and aggregate downloads

HM Land Registry PPD; collected valuation-list records; official local Band D charges and stock shares; retained ONS geographical references.
